Turn Off Wrist Detection because of a tattoo: is it worth it?

Turning off Wrist Detection (the feature many call pulse or wrist detection) stops Apple Watch from asking for the passcode on its own, but also turns off heart rate, sleep and other automatic measurements, and the watch no longer locks by itself. For most people, another solution is worth trying first.

Why the watch asks for a passcode with a tattoo

When wrist detection fails, Apple Watch may treat the device as off-wrist and ask for the passcode again. Details and symptoms are in the guide Apple Watch asking for a passcode with a tattoo.

What Wrist Detection does

It tells the watch that it is on your arm. With the feature on and a passcode set, Apple Watch locks when it leaves your wrist and only unlocks with the passcode. Apple describes the feature and what changes when you turn it off in the support article If your Apple Watch locks unexpectedly.

What you lose when you turn it off

According to Apple Support, when you turn off Wrist Detection you lose, among others:

  • Automatic lock and unlock when you take the watch off or put it on
  • Fall Detection starting Emergency SOS on its own
  • Heart rate tracking and notifications
  • Background blood oxygen and respiratory rate measurements
  • Sleep tracking
  • Some Activity measurements
  • Noise measurement and alerts
  • Tap and wrist-turn gestures

Apple Pay still works, but asks for the passcode on every payment (when you double-press the side button to authorize).

Alternatives

Apple’s first recommendation when there is a tattoo or scar on the wrist you use is to move the watch to the other arm. After that, update the side on the watch itself in Settings → General → Orientation (Left or Right).

How to turn it off (if you decide to)

On Apple Watch: open Settings → Passcode → Wrist Detection and turn it off. Confirm with your passcode. On iPhone, the equivalent path is in the Watch app, under Passcode (the label may vary by system version). Keep the watch passcode on and re-enable the feature when you can.

Step-by-step and the official list of affected features: support.apple.com/en-us/111819.

When turning it off makes sense

As a temporary fix: for a short time, in a safe environment, accepting passcode entry for Apple Pay and losing automatic heart-rate tracking and other features. As a long-term solution, it is worth evaluating the alternatives above — including the guide on Apple Watch and tattoos.

FAQ

Does turning off Wrist Detection wipe my data?

No. It only changes how the watch decides it is on your arm and which features tied to that detection stay active.

Does Apple Pay work with detection turned off?

Yes: payment stays available, but you must enter the passcode when you double-press the side button to authorize.

Can a light tattoo also cause the problem?

It usually interferes less. Light, low-density pigments let more sensor light through; still, each skin and design reacts differently.

Does this apply to Samsung, Garmin and other brands?

The principle of optical sensors is similar, but the feature name and options change by model. This guide covers Apple Watch.

Does the lens interfere with charging?

It is designed not to interfere with inductive charging. Use the charger recommended for your model and wait for the adhesive cure time in the manual before getting it wet.
